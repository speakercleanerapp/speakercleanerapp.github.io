How the speaker cleaner works

A phone speaker is a tiny diaphragm attached to a coil that sits in a magnetic field. When an audio signal reaches the coil, the diaphragm moves in and out and pushes the air in front of it, which you hear as sound. Directly in front of the diaphragm is a mesh grille, and that mesh is exactly where water and fine dust like to sit. A single drop stretched across the mesh is enough to make music sound muffled, buzzy or quieter than usual.

Low frequencies make the diaphragm travel much further than high ones at the same loudness. When this page plays a low tone, the diaphragm acts like a small air pump. Each push forces air through the grille, and the repeated pressure changes break the surface tension holding a droplet in place, so the water is shaken loose and pushed out of the opening. Loose dust and lint near the surface of the mesh can be dislodged the same way.

The tones are created on the fly using the Web Audio API that is built into every modern browser. No audio file is downloaded and the microphone is never used. Because the tone is generated as a pure sine wave, it is clean and predictable, with smooth fades at the start and end to avoid pops that could stress the speaker.

The sweep works because every speaker and grille combination has a slightly different resonance. Gliding through a range of frequencies makes sure the speaker hits the point where it moves the most air for your particular phone. The pulse stage adds sudden starts and stops, which are good at jolting a droplet that a steady tone only wobbles.

How to clean an iPhone speaker

  1. Dry the outside of the phone with a soft, lint-free cloth. Remove any case so the bottom speaker holes are not covered.
  2. Make sure silent mode is off (the ring/silent switch or Action button), and disconnect AirPods or any Bluetooth speaker in Control Center.
  3. Turn the media volume up with the side buttons while audio is not playing, or after you tap Start.
  4. Choose Water eject or Auto clean cycle and tap Start. Safari only allows sound to begin after a tap, so if nothing plays, tap Stop and Start once more.
  5. Hold the phone with the bottom edge facing down so gravity helps. For the earpiece speaker at the top, turn the phone upside down and run the cycle again.
  6. When it finishes, wipe away any droplets that appeared around the grille, then play some music to check the sound.

How to clean an Android phone speaker

  1. Wipe the phone dry and take off the case. Many Android phones have one speaker on the bottom edge and a second in the earpiece.
  2. Open this page in Chrome, Samsung Internet, Firefox or any modern browser.
  3. Raise the media volume, not the ringtone volume. On most phones you press a volume key, then expand the slider panel to see media.
  4. Pick a mode and tap Start. Point the bottom speaker downward during the first run.
  5. Run a second pass with the phone flipped to target the earpiece speaker if it also sounds muffled.
  6. Some phones show a moisture warning on the charging port. Do not charge until that warning clears, even if the speaker sounds fine again.

Earbuds and headphones: be careful

Earbuds, including AirPods and other wireless buds, have much smaller drivers than a phone speaker and are designed to sit at very low volume right inside your ear. If you want to try the tool on them, take them out of your ears first, put them on a table with the speaker mesh facing down, start at a low to medium level, and run only the 30-second water eject mode. Stop immediately if you hear crackling. Never wear headphones while any cleaning tone is playing, because a loud continuous low tone can be uncomfortable and harmful to hearing. For most earbuds, gentle drying with a cloth and a few hours of air drying with the case open is the safest approach.

Common speaker problems and what to do

Most muffled phone speakers fall into a handful of everyday situations. Knowing which one you are dealing with helps you pick the right mode and decide whether sound alone is enough.

Caught in the rain

Rain usually leaves a thin film of fresh water over the bottom grille. Shake the phone gently with the speaker facing down, dry the outside, then run the water eject tone once. Fresh rainwater evaporates cleanly, so one or two passes plus an hour of air drying normally restores full sound.

Steam from the shower or kitchen

Taking a phone into a steamy bathroom lets warm vapour condense inside the grille and even behind it. The sound may seem fine at first and turn dull later as droplets gather. Move the phone to a cooler, dry room, wait ten minutes for condensation to settle, then run the auto clean cycle. Avoid doing this repeatedly, because steam can work past seals that resist splashes.

Pocket lint and fluff

Lint builds up slowly, so the speaker gets quieter over weeks rather than suddenly. Use a soft, dry brush to lift fibres from the surface first, then run the dust cleaner to shake loose any particles caught in the mesh. Sound cannot pull out a tightly packed plug of lint, so the brush step matters.

Sand after a beach day

Sand grains are heavier than dust and often mixed with salt water. Tap the phone lightly against your palm with the speaker facing down, brush the grille, and only then use sound. If the phone went into the sea, follow your manufacturer's advice about rinsing with fresh water before trying any tone, since dried salt can corrode contacts.

Muffled calls but normal music

If music sounds fine but the person on a call sounds distant, the problem is probably the earpiece speaker at the top of the screen, not the main speaker. Flip the phone so the earpiece faces down and run the water eject or vibration cleaner mode. If callers say you sound muffled, the microphone holes are blocked instead; brush them gently, because tones do not affect microphones.

Only one side sounds wrong

Phones with stereo speakers can be blocked on one side only. Use the speaker selector above to send the tone to just the left or right channel, and check the result with the speaker test. Hold the phone in landscape so you know which physical speaker is which.

When a speaker cleaner sound will not help

Sound can only move what is loose. It is worth being realistic about what it cannot do:

  • Physical damage. A speaker that crackles at every volume, cuts out, or makes no sound at all after a drop may have a torn diaphragm or a broken connection. No tone can repair that.
  • Corrosion. Salt water, sugary drinks and chlorinated water can leave residue that dries on the coil and contacts. Over days this can corrode metal parts. Rinse residue promptly according to your phone maker's guidance and seek repair if sound gets worse.
  • Packed debris. Pocket lint and dried grime pressed deep into the mesh usually need gentle mechanical cleaning with a soft, dry brush, held at an angle and never pushed inward.
  • Software issues. If sound is missing everywhere, restart the phone, check Bluetooth routing and accessibility audio settings, and update the system before assuming the speaker is blocked.
  • Water inside the body. If the phone was submerged for a long time, water may be inside the device, not just in the grille. Switch it off and let it dry; get it checked if it behaves oddly.

Safety tips

  • Keep the phone at arm's length while the tone plays. Low tones can sound quieter than they really are.
  • Do not push cotton buds, pins, or toothpicks into the speaker holes. They can puncture the mesh or the waterproof membrane behind it.
  • Skip hair dryers, ovens and other heat sources. Heat can warp seals and damage the battery.
  • Avoid compressed air aimed straight into the grille; strong blasts can push water deeper or damage the membrane.
  • Do not charge a phone that still shows a liquid warning or that you suspect is wet inside.
  • Take breaks between runs. Two or three cycles is enough; running tones for many minutes will not speed up drying.
  • Remember that water resistance ratings wear down over time and do not cover warm water, soapy water or the sea.

Frequently asked questions

Does a speaker cleaner sound really work?

It can help when the problem is a few droplets of water or loose dust sitting in the speaker grille. A low tone makes the speaker diaphragm move a long way back and forth, which pushes air through the grille and nudges trapped water out. It cannot repair a torn or corroded speaker.

Why is 165 Hz used for water eject?

Around 150 to 170 Hz is low enough to create large diaphragm movement but still high enough for the small speakers in phones to reproduce loudly. That combination moves the most air, which is what pushes water out.

How loud should the volume be?

Turn the phone's media volume to around 70 to 100 percent and keep the in-page slider moderate at first. If the sound starts to rattle harshly or distort, lower it a little. Never hold the phone to your ear while the tone is playing.

How many times can I run the cleaning cycle?

Two or three runs with short breaks in between is plenty. If the speaker still sounds muffled after that, let the phone dry naturally for several hours before trying again.

Is it safe for AirPods or other earbuds?

Use extra care. Earbuds are not designed for long loud tones, and you must never wear them while the tone plays. Take them out, set the volume low to medium, and run only short sessions. Many earbud makers recommend simply drying them with a lint-free cloth and leaving them to air dry.

Why can't I hear anything on my iPhone?

Check that the ring/silent switch or Action button is not set to silent, raise the media volume, and make sure no Bluetooth headphones are connected. Then tap Start again; iOS only allows web audio to begin after a tap.

Do I need to install an app?

No. The tones are generated live in your browser with the Web Audio API. Nothing is downloaded, recorded or uploaded.

Should I put my wet phone in rice?

Rice is not recommended by major phone makers. It absorbs moisture slowly and can leave starch dust in ports. Leaving the phone in a dry place with good airflow works better.

Can I clean only the left or right speaker?

Yes. Choose Left, Right or Both in the speaker selector before or during a run. On phones with a single speaker, both channels are mixed into that one speaker, so the choice makes no difference there.

Does the vibration option help remove water?

On Android phones that support web vibration, the motor adds a gentle shake that can help droplets move toward the grille opening while the tone plays. iPhones do not let websites use the vibration motor, so on iOS the option is turned off and the tone works on its own.

How long should I wait before charging after water exposure?

Wait until the phone is completely dry and any liquid detection warning has cleared. For a light splash that may be under an hour; after a dunk, leaving it several hours in a dry, airy spot is safer. Charging a wet port can damage it.

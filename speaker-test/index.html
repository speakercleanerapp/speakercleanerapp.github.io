Why test your speakers

After cleaning, the best way to know whether your speaker is fully back to normal is to listen to it with a predictable signal. Music is complicated and can hide problems, while a clean test tone makes rattles, buzzing and missing channels obvious. This page offers two tests: a left / right channel test that plays a tone from each side in turn, and a frequency sweep that glides from 50 Hz up to 16 kHz so you can hear how your speaker handles low, middle and high pitches.

How the left / right test works

The channel test plays a 440 Hz tone for three seconds on the left channel, three seconds on the right, and three seconds on both. It then repeats the pattern at 660 Hz. The display shows which channel is active at every moment, so you can compare what you hear with what should be playing.

  • Stereo phones: Hold the phone in landscape. One speaker is usually at the bottom edge and the other is combined with the earpiece. Depending on how you rotate the phone, the left channel may come from either end, because many phones swap channels to match orientation.
  • Single-speaker phones: You will hear the tone during all three parts because the phone mixes both channels into one speaker. That is normal.
  • Headphones and laptops: The test is a quick way to confirm left and right are wired correctly. Keep the volume low with headphones and never use the cleaning modes while wearing them.

How the frequency sweep works

The sweep starts at 50 Hz and rises smoothly to 16,000 Hz over 24 seconds, with the current frequency shown live in the tool. Small phone speakers cannot reproduce the very lowest notes, so the beginning may be faint or silent. The sound should then grow clearer through the middle range and finish with a thin, high whistle. Many adults cannot hear the final few seconds above 14–15 kHz; that is a normal part of how hearing changes with age, not a speaker fault.

The sweep uses the speaker selector, so you can test the left speaker, the right speaker or both separately.

Step-by-step: test after cleaning

  1. Let the phone sit for a few minutes after any cleaning run so loosened water can drain.
  2. Set the media volume to about 50 percent. A test does not need to be loud.
  3. Select Left / right test and tap Start. Confirm you hear each side when the label says so.
  4. Select Frequency sweep, choose Both, and tap Start. Listen for buzzing or sudden drops in volume.
  5. If one side sounds worse, choose that side in the selector and repeat the sweep to compare.
  6. Go back to the cleaning tools for another pass if needed, then test again.

Settings that can affect the test

Some phone settings change how channels are played. Mono audio in accessibility settings combines left and right, so both speakers play the same thing. Audio enhancers, equalisers and "Dolby" style effects can change the loudness of certain pitches. Bluetooth devices take over the output entirely, so disconnect them first. On iPhone, silent mode must be off for web audio to play through the speaker.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I hear the left tone from both speakers?

Your phone probably has a single speaker, or Mono audio is enabled in accessibility settings. In both cases the two channels are mixed together.

Why are the left and right sides swapped?

Many stereo phones swap channels when you rotate them so the sound matches the screen orientation. Try turning the phone the other way.

I cannot hear the end of the frequency sweep. Is my speaker broken?

Probably not. Many adults cannot hear above about 14 to 15 kHz, and some phone speakers roll off at the highest pitches.

Why is the start of the sweep so quiet?

Small phone speakers cannot reproduce deep bass around 50 to 80 Hz with much volume. The sound should become clear above about 150 Hz.

Is the speaker test safe for headphones?

At low volume, yes. Turn the volume down before starting, because a pure tone can sound louder than music. Never use cleaning modes while wearing headphones.

Can I test only one speaker?

Yes. Choose Left or Right in the speaker selector before running the frequency sweep. The channel test always cycles through left, right and both.

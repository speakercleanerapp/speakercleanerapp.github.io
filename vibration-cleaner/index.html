How a vibration cleaner works

Every phone contains a small vibration motor, sometimes called a haptic engine. It is the part that buzzes for notifications. When it runs, the whole body of the phone shakes slightly. That shake is a different kind of motion from the speaker's own movement: the speaker pumps air through the grille, while the motor moves the entire grille back and forth like a sieve being tapped.

The vibration cleaner combines both. It plays rhythmic low-frequency pulses through the speaker and, on browsers that allow it, triggers the vibration motor in a repeating on-off pattern at the same time. The pulses push air in short bursts, and the body vibration helps droplets and loose particles move toward the opening instead of sitting still. The routine runs for 40 seconds in three stages: slow pulses at 130 Hz, a rumble sweep from 70 to 200 Hz, and fast pulses at 165 Hz.

Which phones support web vibration

Websites can only use the vibration motor through the browser's Vibration API, and support varies:

  • Android: Chrome, Edge, Samsung Internet, Firefox and most other Android browsers support it. The phone must not be in a mode that disables vibration, and some battery-saver settings turn it off.
  • iPhone and iPad: Apple does not allow websites to control the vibration motor in Safari or in any other iOS browser. On these devices the vibration checkbox is turned off automatically and the tone still plays normally.
  • Laptops and desktops: There is no vibration motor, so the option has no effect. The tone works as usual.

If the checkbox is available but you feel nothing, check that vibration is enabled in the phone's sound and vibration settings and that Do Not Disturb is not muting haptics.

Step-by-step: use the vibration cleaner

  1. Remove the case. Thick cases absorb much of the vibration and can trap water against the grille.
  2. Turn the media volume up to 70–100 percent and disconnect any Bluetooth audio device.
  3. Make sure Vibrate with tone is ticked. It is selected by default on this page when your browser supports it.
  4. Rest the phone on a soft towel with the speaker edge pointing down and slightly over the edge, or hold it loosely in your hand. A loose grip lets the phone shake more freely.
  5. Tap Start and let the full 40-second cycle run.
  6. Wipe the grille, then run the water eject or dust cleaner mode if the speaker still sounds dull.

When vibration helps most

Droplets stuck at the edge of the grille

Sometimes a droplet clears from the centre of the mesh but clings to the rim of the opening. The speaker's air movement is weakest there, while body vibration affects the whole edge equally.

Earpiece speakers

The earpiece is quieter than the main speaker and moves less air. Adding vibration gives it extra help. Turn the phone upside down so the earpiece faces the floor.

Crumbs and grains on the surface

Small solid particles such as sand or crumbs resting on top of the grille are easily shaken off by the motor, then cleared by a final brush.

Limits and cautions

Vibration does not reach deep inside the speaker chamber and cannot remove water that has gone beyond the waterproof membrane. It also uses a little more battery. Running the motor continuously for long periods is not useful; one or two cycles is enough, followed by air drying. Do not place the phone on a hard table edge where vibration could make it slide off, and keep it away from your ear while the tone is playing.

If your phone shows a liquid detection warning, leave it unplugged until the warning clears. If you notice that the vibration feels weaker than usual or makes a grinding noise, the motor itself may need service, and that is unrelated to the speaker.

Combining vibration with other modes

You can tick Vibrate with tone on any mode, not only the vibration preset. Pairing it with the 165 Hz water eject tone is a good second pass for stubborn water. For one-sided problems on stereo phones, choose the left or right speaker in the selector so the tone goes where it is needed while the whole phone vibrates.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the vibration option greyed out on my iPhone?

iOS does not let websites use the vibration motor in any browser. The tone itself still works and is the part that pushes water out of the grille.

Can vibration damage my phone?

No. The pattern uses the same motor that buzzes for calls and notifications, running in short pulses. It only stops when the cycle ends or you tap Stop.

Does vibration alone remove water?

Rarely. Body vibration helps droplets move, but the speaker's air movement does most of the work. That is why this mode always plays a tone as well.

Why do I not feel any vibration on Android?

Check that vibration is enabled in sound settings, that Do Not Disturb or battery saver is not blocking haptics, and that you started the tool with a tap.

Can I vibrate with the dust or water mode?

Yes. Tick Vibrate with tone before starting any mode. The vibration pattern runs alongside the selected tone.

Is it OK to use the vibration cleaner on earbuds?

No. Earbuds have no vibration motor that a website can reach, and you should never wear them while a cleaning tone plays.

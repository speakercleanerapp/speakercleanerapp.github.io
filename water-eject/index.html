What the water eject tone does

When a phone gets wet, water rarely goes deep into the device straight away. Most of it collects in the small chamber between the speaker grille and the speaker itself. That chamber is shallow and the mesh holes are tiny, so surface tension holds a droplet in place like a drum skin stretched across the opening. While it sits there, sound has to push through the water as well as the air, which is why everything sounds muffled, crackly or oddly quiet.

The water eject tone on this page plays a steady sine wave at 165 Hz. At that pitch a phone speaker's diaphragm swings through a large distance on every cycle, 165 times per second. Each swing compresses and then rarefies the air in the chamber, and that pumping action is what breaks the droplet's grip on the mesh. As the water film is shaken apart, small drops are pushed out through the grille where you can wipe them away.

Why 165 Hz instead of a lower bass note

Lower notes would move the diaphragm even further, but phone speakers are tiny and cannot reproduce deep bass with much force. Below roughly 100 Hz most of the energy is lost and you mainly hear distortion. Higher notes are easier for the speaker to play loudly, but the diaphragm moves less, so it pushes less air. The range from about 150 to 180 Hz is a practical sweet spot: loud enough on almost every phone, and low enough to produce strong air movement. We settled on 165 Hz because it sits comfortably in the middle of that band and sounds clean on both small and larger speakers.

If a single pitch does not clear the speaker, the auto clean cycle preset adds sweeps and pulses around that frequency. Different phones resonate at slightly different pitches, and gliding through a range makes sure your particular speaker hits its strongest point at least once.

Step-by-step: eject water from your phone

  1. Switch off and dry the outside if it was submerged. For a splash, simply wipe it. For a full dunk, power down first, dry it with a lint-free cloth, then turn it on again once the outside is dry.
  2. Remove the case. Many cases have narrow cut-outs that trap water against the grille.
  3. Set volume high. Raise the media volume to 70–100 percent. On iPhone, make sure silent mode is off. Disconnect Bluetooth headphones or speakers so the sound comes from the phone.
  4. Point the speaker down. Hold the phone so the grille faces the floor. Gravity helps the loosened water fall out instead of settling back.
  5. Tap Start. The 165 Hz tone runs for 30 seconds. You may see fine droplets or mist appear at the grille, especially in the first ten seconds.
  6. Wipe and repeat. Dry the grille, then run it once or twice more. Switch to the auto clean cycle if the sound is still dull.
  7. Do the earpiece too. Turn the phone upside down so the top speaker faces down and run another pass. This is the speaker you hear during calls.
  8. Let it air dry. Leave the phone in a dry room with good airflow for an hour or more before judging the final result.

How to tell it worked

Play a song you know well or a spoken podcast at medium volume. Clear speakers produce crisp consonants like "s" and "t" in speech, and cymbals in music sound bright rather than smeared. If you still hear a faint buzz on bass notes, a droplet may be partially attached; another 30-second pass usually finishes the job. If the sound is perfect at low volume but rattles at high volume, give the phone more drying time, because some moisture may be in the speaker's back chamber where tones reach less effectively.

Fresh water, salt water and other liquids

Tap water and rainwater are the easiest case: once pushed out, what remains simply evaporates. Pool water contains chlorine, sea water contains salt, and drinks contain sugar or acids. When those liquids dry they leave residue that can stick the mesh together or slowly corrode metal. For anything other than fresh water, check your phone maker's guidance; some recommend a brief rinse with clean tap water before drying. Run the tone afterwards to clear the rinse water as well.

Things that make water damage worse

  • Charging while wet. Current flowing through a damp port can cause corrosion and damage the connector. Wait for any liquid warning to clear.
  • Heat. Hair dryers, radiators and sunny windowsills can soften adhesives that keep the phone sealed.
  • Shaking hard. A firm flick can drive water deeper. A few gentle shakes with the speaker down is enough.
  • Poking the grille. Pins and cotton buds can tear the mesh or the waterproof membrane behind it.
  • Rice. It is slow, leaves starch dust and does not draw water out of the speaker chamber.

Using left and right speaker selection

Many recent phones have two speakers that act as a stereo pair when held sideways: one at the bottom edge and one combined with the earpiece. If only one of them sounds wet, choose Left or Right in the tool so all the energy goes to that speaker. Hold the phone in landscape to see which side is which. You can change the selection while the tone is running. On single-speaker phones both channels play from the same speaker.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I play the water eject tone?

Start with one 30-second run, wipe the grille, and repeat up to three times. If the sound is still muffled, let the phone air dry for a few hours rather than playing the tone for longer.

Is 165 Hz safe for my phone speaker?

Yes, at normal volume. 165 Hz is well within the range phone speakers are built to play, and the tone fades in and out to avoid sudden pops. Lower the tone level if you hear harsh rattling.

Why do I see mist instead of drops?

Very small amounts of water are broken into a fine spray by the fast-moving diaphragm. That is a good sign the water is leaving the grille.

Does this work for the earpiece speaker?

Yes. Turn the phone so the top speaker faces down and run the tone again. Earpieces are quieter, so use a higher volume and expect it to take a little longer.

My phone was in the sea. Will this be enough?

The tone removes water from the grille, but salt left behind can cause corrosion. Follow your manufacturer's rinse and drying advice and get the phone checked if any feature behaves oddly afterwards.

Can I use the tone with a wet phone that is charging?

No. Unplug it first. Charging a wet phone risks damaging the port, and the tone works just as well on battery.
